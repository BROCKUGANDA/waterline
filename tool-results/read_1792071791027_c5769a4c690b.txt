    20→PART 1
    21→Product definition
    22→WATERLINE is a liquidity layer embedded in du Business that converts approved invoices and
    23→idle balances into usable cash, priced deterministically and settled over licensed rails.
    24→1.1 Problem statement
    25→A UAE SME holding a buyer-approved invoice waits 45 to 90 days for cash it has already earned. The capital is not at
    26→risk and the amount is not in dispute; it is queuing. During the queue the business cannot act on opportunity or absorb
    27→shock. Conventional remedies — a bank facility or factoring — require the business to prove creditworthiness it has
    28→already demonstrated, on a timeline that misses the opportunity. The addressable inefficiency is the queue itself, not
    29→the SME's access to credit.
    30→1.2 Users and jobs to be done
    31→PERSONA CONTEXT JOB TO BE DONE
    32→SME owner-operator 1–20 staff, no finance function, manages cash "Tell me what I can safely spend this week,
    33→from a phone between operational tasks. and let me get paid now when I need to
    34→move."
    35→SME finance lead 20–200 staff, part-time or single-person finance "Give me a reliable forward view and let me
    36→function, uses accounting software and close the gap without starting a loan
    37→spreadsheets. application."
    38→Anchor buyer AP clerk Enterprise accounts payable, processes "Let me confirm invoices in bulk and
    39→hundreds of invoices weekly, measured on capture discounts without adding steps to
    40→cycle time and error rate. my process."
    41→du operations analyst Monitors the funding book, resolves exceptions, "Show me what needs a human, why, and
    42→answers SME queries. give me the authority to resolve it with a
    43→trail."
    44→Risk and compliance officer Accountable to CBUAE and internal audit for "Prove to me that this decision was made
    45→every automated decision. by a rule I approved, and replay it on
    46→demand."
    47→Funder credit officer Deploys bank or fund capital against the "Show me loss performance by cohort,
    48→receivables book. concentration, and the controls that bound
    49→my downside."
    50→1.3 Scope
    51→In scope, Phase 1 Dynamic discounting on buyer-approved receivables; milestone escrow via programmable
    52→mandate at a licensed bank; treasury autopilot into low-risk short-term instruments; cash-flow
    53→forecasting; buyer confirmation portal; dispute workflow; operator console; SME mobile and
    54→web experience embedded in du Business; Arabic and RTL.
    55→In scope, Phase 2 Open Finance account information and payment initiation at depth; e-invoice verification at
    56→source; multi-funder allocation engine; anchor-buyer ERP connectors; supplier-initiated early
    57→payment requests.
    58→Phase 3, flagged Tokenised recurring revenue for upfront capital, pending CBUAE and VARA guidance. Fully
    59→specified, feature-flagged, zero dependency from Phases 1 and 2.

    62→===== PAGE 3 =====
    63→Explicitly out of scope Unsecured lending of any kind; consumer credit; FX and cross-border payments; crypto
    64→custody or trading; insurance; any write path into du BSS; any participation in rating or
    65→charging.
    66→1.4 Product principles
    67→No model decides who gets paid. Machine learning forecasts and ranks; a deterministic versioned rule pack
    68→decides and prices. Always.
    69→Every decision is replayable. Input hash, rule-pack version, resolved parameters, outcome, plain-language
    70→reason — written append-only at decision time, not reconstructed.
    71→Show the arithmetic. The SME sees days early, rate, fee in dirhams and net proceeds before accepting, and the
    72→same numbers afterwards.
    73→Degrade, never fail. Five declared modes. Partial service with honest status beats a blank screen.
    74→The SME's floor is absolute. Treasury autopilot never breaches the minimum balance the SME sets, under any
    75→optimisation.
    76→Exceptions surface, never silently fail. An SME must never learn about a problem by noticing money has not
    77→arrived.
    78→One binary. Pilot and full base run identical code behind flags. No pilot build to throw away.

    84→2.1 Service topology
    85→Twelve services, each independently deployable, communicating over authenticated internal APIs for synchronous
    86→work and an event log for asynchronous work. All services on the funding path are stateless.
    87→SERVICE RESPONSIBILITY PATH
    88→identity SME and staff identity, UAE Pass and du SSO federation, session, entitlement, role. Hot
    89→consent Scoped, revocable consent capture; purpose registry; enforcement hooks; revocation Hot
    90→propagation.
    91→registry Entities, trade licences, UBO, bank accounts, buyer relationships, KYB state. Warm
    92→invoice Invoice ingestion, normalisation to the Peppol model, hashing, duplicate detection, Warm
    93→verification state machine.
    94→ruleengine Deterministic evaluation of the active rule pack. Pure function: no I/O during evaluation, no Hot
    95→clock access, no randomness.
    96→forecast Cash-flow forecasting, obligation detection, risk ranking, anomaly scoring. Advisory only; Warm
    97→produces features, never decisions.
    98→decision Orchestrates a decision: assembles features, calls ruleengine, writes the immutable Hot
    99→decision record, emits the event.
   100→ledger Double-entry ledger of record. Every movement is a balanced transaction; balances are Hot
   101→derived, never stored as truth.
   102→payout Money movement orchestration over du Pay and Aani. Idempotent, signed, reconciled. Hot
   103→escrow Milestone contract lifecycle, condition evaluation, release instruction to the custodian bank. Warm
   104→treasury Sweep and recall engine, floor enforcement, obligation calendar, instrument allocation. Warm
   105→audit Append-only decision and event store, retention, legal hold, regulatory retrieval. Cold
   106→2.2 Hot, warm and cold paths
   107→Separating paths by latency budget is what keeps analytics load from ever contending with a payout.
   108→PATH BUDGET AND STORAGE CARRIES
   109→Hot p99 under 400 ms. In-memory cache Authentication, balance display, limit and price
   110→plus primary Postgres. Served active- quote, offer acceptance, payout initiation, status.
   111→active from both zones.
   112→Warm Seconds to minutes. Queue-driven, Invoice verification, buyer confirmation, forecast
   113→primary Postgres plus search index. refresh, escrow condition evaluation, sweep
   114→calculation, reconciliation, notification.
   115→Cold Minutes to hours. Object storage, Backtesting, shadow pricing, portfolio analytics,
   116→columnar warehouse, immutable audit regulatory retrieval, statement generation, model
   117→store. training.
   118→A quote is served hot from precomputed eligibility and a cached rule pack. Nothing on the hot path calls an external
   119→provider synchronously: if fresh bank data is needed, the quote is served from last-known-good with an explicit
   120→freshness indicator, and the refresh happens warm.

   124→2.3 Multi-tenancy and partitioning
   125→Tenant key on every row. Enforced by Postgres row-level security, not by application discipline. A query without
   126→tenant context returns nothing.
   127→Hash partitioning of high-volume tables (invoice, decision, ledger_entry, event) by tenant, so account growth
   128→does not degrade per-account performance.
   129→Per-tenant quotas on request rate, concurrent decisions and queue depth, so no single tenant can consume
   130→shared capacity.
   131→Connection pooling per partition group with bulkheads, so saturation in one group cannot exhaust the pool for
   132→others.
   133→2.4 Active-active
   134→Two UAE availability zones serve live traffic simultaneously. Both are primary for reads and writes on the hot path.
   135→Data Synchronous replication for the ledger and decision store — correctness over latency, no
   136→exceptions. Asynchronous replication for search, cache and analytics.
   137→Conflict policy Money movement is partitioned by tenant to a home zone, so two zones never write the same
   138→ledger account concurrently. On failover the home zone is reassigned, never shared.
   139→Objectives RPO zero for ledger and decision records. RTO 15 minutes for full service, 2 minutes for
   140→degraded read-only.
   141→Drill cadence Zone failover drilled monthly in staging and quarterly in production during a declared window,
   142→with the result published internally. An undrilled failover is assumed broken.

   183→PART 3
   184→The rule pack
   185→The rule pack is the single place where eligibility, limits and prices are decided. It is data, not
   186→code, and it is versioned, signed, replayable and promotable independently of any deployment.
   187→3.1 Structure
   188→rulepack:
   189→version: "2026.10.3" # immutable once promoted
   190→signed_by: "risk-committee"
   191→effective_from: "2026-10-15T00:00:00Z"
   192→eligibility:
   193→- id: E01 when: entity.kyb_state != 'verified' then: reject(E_KYB)
   194→- id: E02 when: entity.licence_expiry < today + 30d then: reject(E_LICENCE)
   195→- id: E03 when: invoice.verification_state != 'verified' then: reject(E_UNVERIFIED)
   196→- id: E04 when: invoice.due_date < today then: reject(E_OVERDUE)
   197→- id: E05 when: invoice.gross_amount < 2000 AED then: reject(E_MIN_SIZE)
   198→- id: E06 when: buyer_link.tier == 'unrated'
   199→and buyer_link.confirmed_invoice_count < 3 then: cap(advance_rate, 0.60)
   200→limits:
   201→base: min(0.90 * invoice.gross, entity_cap, buyer_cap, sector_cap)
   202→entity_cap: f(tenure_months, billing_behaviour_band, confirmed_history)
   203→buyer_cap: f(buyer_tier, avg_days_to_pay, dispute_rate)
   204→sector_cap: table(sector_code)
   205→portfolio: hard_stop if book_exposure > portfolio_ceiling
   206→pricing:
   207→rate_bps_per_day: base_curve(buyer_tier) + risk_adj(features) + funding_cost
   208→floor_bps: 1.2 ceiling_bps: 9.0
   209→fee = net_days_early * rate_bps_per_day * principal / 10000
   210→explanations:
   211→E_KYB: "We need your trade licence verified before we can price this."
   212→E_LICENCE: "Your trade licence expires within 30 days. Renew to continue."
   213→E_UNVERIFIED: "Your buyer hasn't confirmed this invoice yet."
   214→...
   215→3.2 Determinism guarantees
   216→Pure evaluation. ruleengine performs no I/O, reads no clock and uses no randomness. Time and all features
   217→are passed in as an explicit, hashed feature snapshot.
   218→Hashed inputs. The feature snapshot is canonicalised and hashed; the hash is stored on the decision. Replaying
   219→the same hash against the same pack version must produce a byte-identical outcome, and this is asserted in CI.
   220→No silent fallback. A missing feature is a typed rejection with a reason code, never a default value substituted
   221→quietly.
   222→Decimal arithmetic. All money is integer minor units; all rates are fixed-point. No floating point touches a
   223→monetary calculation anywhere in the system.
   224→3.3 Promotion lifecycle
   225→1 — Author Change proposed as a diff against the active pack, with rationale and expected population
   226→impact.
   227→2 — Shadow Candidate evaluated against 12 months of history and 30 days of live traffic in parallel.
   228→Outcome deltas reported by segment: approvals gained and lost, price moves, limit moves,
   229→expected loss change.
   230→WATERLINE — PRD, Design System & Master Build Specification · du SME Challenge, Theme 2 7
   231→

   233→3 — Review Risk committee signs the pack. Fairness dispersion across sector, size and emirate is
   234→reviewed as a required gate.
   235→4 — Canary Promoted to 5% of tenants for 72 hours with automatic rollback on guardrail breach.
   236→5 — Promote Full promotion. The prior pack remains loadable forever for replay; nothing is ever deleted.
   237→Rollback Pointer change to the previous version, effective within 60 seconds, requiring no deployment.

   252→PART 4
   253→Core flows
   254→4.1 Onboarding and consent
   255→Target: under three minutes, zero documents uploaded in the standard path.
   256→01 Entry
   257→SME taps WATERLINE in du Business. Already authenticated via du SSO; no new credential is created.
   258→02 Identity
   259→UAE Pass step-up for the principal. Trade licence and establishment card verified against registry data. KYB
   260→state moves to verified or into a typed exception queue.
   261→03 Consent
   262→Scoped consent presented in plain language, one purpose per toggle: billing history, bank data via Open
   263→Finance, buyer confirmation contact. Each is independently revocable and each shows exactly what it
   264→unlocks.
   265→04 First value
   266→Before any invoice is uploaded, the SME sees its indicative limit derived from du billing history alone. This is
   267→the activation moment and it requires nothing from the user.
   268→Revocation propagates within 60 seconds: dependent features disable immediately, derived features are purged
   269→within 24 hours, and existing funded positions continue to their contractual conclusion with that fact disclosed at
   270→revocation time.
   271→4.2 Invoice verification state machine
   272→received ──normalise──> parsed ──hash──> deduped
   273→deduped ──einvoice match──> verified
   274→deduped ──buyer confirm──> verified
   275→deduped ──du AP file match──> verified
   276→deduped ──no route──> unverifiable(reason)
   277→parsed ──hash collision──> duplicate(ref to original)
   278→verified ──buyer disputes──> disputed ──resolve──> verified | written_off
   279→verified ──buyer amends──> superseded(new invoice id)
   280→Hashing Canonical hash over buyer tax ID, supplier tax ID, invoice number, gross amount and issue
   281→date. Resistant to reformatting and whitespace, sensitive to any material change.
   282→Cross-tenant check The hash index is global. The same receivable presented by two tenants is caught even
   283→though the data is tenant-isolated, with the collision routed to fraud review and never auto-
   284→rejected.
   285→Verification priority E-invoice registry, then du AP file, then buyer API callback, then buyer portal single-click.
   286→Weaker routes carry a lower advance-rate cap set in the rule pack.

   289→===== PAGE 10 =====
   290→4.3 Pricing, acceptance and payout
   291→01 Quote
   292→decision assembles the feature snapshot, calls ruleengine, writes the decision record, returns limit and
   293→rate. Budget: p99 under 400 ms, served without any synchronous external call.
   294→02 Model
   295→The SME moves a date slider and sees fee and net proceeds recompute locally against the quoted rate curve.
   296→No server round trip, so the interaction is instant and works on poor connectivity.
   297→03 Accept
   298→Offer accepted with the decision ID and a client idempotency key. The offer is re-validated server-side against
   299→its expiry and the current pack; a changed price is surfaced explicitly and requires fresh acceptance, never
   300→applied silently.
   301→04 Reserve and post
   302→Funding source reserved, assignment recorded, ledger transaction posted. Ledger commit and payout
   303→initiation are bound by an outbox so that a committed ledger entry always has exactly one payout attempt.
   304→05 Settle
   305→Payout dispatched to du Pay or Aani with the idempotency key. Terminal success, terminal failure or timeout;
   306→timeouts are resolved by reconciliation rather than by retrying blindly.
   307→06 Notify and reconcile
   308→SME notified through du's gateway. Daily statement reconciliation to the dirham, with any break escalated
   309→same day.
   310→4.4 Escrow lifecycle
   311→Funds are held at a licensed custodian bank under a programmable mandate. WATERLINE never holds client money.
   312→Award Buyer funds the contract at the custodian. Supplier sees confirmed, locked funds before
   313→starting work — the single most valuable moment in the product.
   314→Milestone conditions Three types: explicit buyer approval, document-evidenced delivery with buyer review window,
   315→and time-based release with buyer veto. Each is a declared condition in the contract,
   316→evaluated by escrow and instructed to the custodian.
   317→Release On condition satisfaction, release is instructed automatically. Buyer inaction inside a declared
   318→review window is itself a declared condition, which removes the most common cause of stalled
   319→milestone payments.
   320→Dispute Either party raises a dispute; the disputed milestone freezes while undisputed milestones
   321→continue. Resolution is logged with evidence and the state transition is auditable.
   322→Termination Unreleased funds return to the buyer under the contract's declared termination terms, with the
   323→full milestone history exportable for any downstream proceeding.
   324→4.5 Treasury autopilot
   325→The SME sets one number: the minimum balance it will never drop below. Everything else is derived.
   326→WATERLINE — PRD, Design System & Master Build Specification · du SME Challenge, Theme 2 10

   328→===== PAGE 11 =====
   329→Obligation calendar Built from payroll cadence, rent, VAT deadlines, recurring supplier payments and du's own
   330→billing cycle, plus any obligation the SME adds manually. Forward-looking, not inferred from
   331→the past alone.
   332→Sweepable amount balance − floor − obligations(horizon) − safety_margin. Safety margin scales
   333→with forecast error for that tenant, so a volatile business is swept more conservatively.
   334→Instruments Low-risk short-term only, with a published maximum maturity and same-day or next-day
   335→liquidity. Instrument eligibility is rule-pack data, so it is reviewable and changeable without a
   336→deployment.
   337→Recall Automatic ahead of every detected obligation with a margin. A recall that would arrive late is
   338→never initiated; the sweep that would have required it is not made in the first place.
   339→Hard guarantee No optimisation, no yield opportunity and no operator override may breach the floor. This is
   340→enforced in ruleengine as a hard stop, and it is the single most heavily tested invariant in
   341→the system.

   345→PART 5
   346→API behaviour
   347→5.1 Conventions
   348→Transport HTTPS only, TLS 1.3 minimum. Internal service-to-service uses mutual TLS with short-lived
   349→certificates issued by an internal CA.
   350→Auth OAuth 2.1 client credentials for partner and service clients; OIDC with UAE Pass step-up for
   351→SME principals. Tokens are audience-scoped, maximum 15-minute lifetime, never passed in
   352→query strings.
   353→Versioning Path major version (/v1/) plus a date-pinned minor via X-API-Version. Breaking changes
   354→require a new major; two majors are supported concurrently for a minimum of 12 months.
   355→Idempotency Idempotency-Key is required on every POST, PUT and PATCH that moves money or
   356→creates a binding obligation. Keys are client-generated UUIDv7, scoped per tenant, retained
   357→24 hours. Replay returns the original response with Idempotent-Replay: true.
   358→Pagination Opaque cursor only; offset pagination is not offered. Default page 50, maximum 200. Cursors
   359→encode a stable sort key and are valid 24 hours.
   360→Rate limits Token bucket per tenant per endpoint class. Responses always carry RateLimit-Limit,
   361→RateLimit-Remaining, RateLimit-Reset. 429 always carries Retry-After.
   362→Errors RFC 9457 application/problem+json with a stable machine code, human detail,
   363→trace_id and, where applicable, retryable: true|false. Error codes are a public
   364→contract and never repurposed.
   365→Time and money All timestamps RFC 3339 UTC with milliseconds. All amounts integer minor units plus explicit
   366→ISO-4217 currency. Never a float, never a bare number.
   367→Tracing traceparent accepted and propagated end to end. Every response returns trace_id,
   368→including errors, so support can locate any transaction from a screenshot.

   410→5.4 Webhooks
   411→Signed with HMAC-SHA256 over timestamp and raw body, in WATERLINE-Signature, with a 5-minute tolerance
   412→window to bound replay.
   413→At-least-once delivery. Consumers must dedupe on event_id, and this requirement is stated in the contract

   426→6.1 Retries and exponential backoff
   427→One retry policy, defined centrally, applied by every client. Per-call overrides are a review finding, not a convenience.
   428→delay(n) = min(cap, base * 2^n) * jitter jitter ~ U(0.5, 1.5) # full jitter
   429→class base cap max_attempts budget retry_on

   431→quote 80ms 1s 2 2s 503,504,timeout
   432→payout_init 250ms 8s 5 45s 502,503,504,timeout
   433→provider_poll 1s 60s 12 15m any non-terminal
   434→webhook_out 5s 1h 14 24h non-2xx
   435→batch_job 30s 15m 8 6h transient only
   436→Full jitter, always Equal-jitter and no-jitter schedules synchronise retries across clients and produce a thundering
   437→herd precisely when a dependency is already struggling.
   438→Retry budget A client may spend at most 10% of its request volume on retries in any 60-second window.
   439→Exceeding the budget opens the circuit rather than amplifying load into a failing dependency.
   440→Never retry non-idempotent A money movement without an idempotency key is not retried under any circumstance. It is
   441→reconciled instead.
   442→Deadline propagation The remaining deadline travels in the request context. A retry that cannot complete within the
   443→deadline is not attempted — it would burn the dependency for a result nobody will read.
   444→6.2 Idempotency and deduplication
   445→Request level Idempotency-Key stored with a fingerprint of the canonicalised request body. Same key and
   446→same fingerprint replays the stored response; same key and different fingerprint returns 409.
   447→In-flight duplicates block on the original rather than racing it.
   448→Ledger level (tenant_id, source_event_id) is unique on ledger_entry. A double-posted event is
   449→rejected by the database, not by hopeful application logic.
   450→Payout level The provider-facing idempotency key is derived deterministically from offer_id, so a retry
   451→after an ambiguous timeout cannot produce a second payment even across a process restart.
   452→Event consumption Consumers write (consumer, event_id) to a processed-events table inside the same
   453→transaction as their side effect. Exactly-once effect from at-least-once delivery.
   454→Invoice level Global hash index catches the same receivable across tenants and across time, including after
   455→reformatting.
   456→Notification level Content hash plus a 10-minute window suppresses duplicate SMS and push, so a retry storm
   457→never becomes a message storm for the SME.

   462→topic partition key ordering DLQ consumers
   463→invoice.submitted tenant_id per tenant yes verify, fraud

   473→change, then published by a relay. A state change without its event, or an event without its state change, is
   474→structurally impossible.
   475→Dead-letter discipline. Every topic has a DLQ. A DLQ that is non-empty for more than 15 minutes pages a
   476→human. DLQ messages are replayable individually or in bulk after a fix, with replay itself idempotent.
   477→Poison-message handling. Three consecutive failures on the same message routes it to the DLQ rather than
   478→blocking the partition. Partition-blocking is the most common cause of silent queue stalls and is designed out.
   479→Backpressure. Queue depth above threshold sheds load at the edge for low-priority work (analytics, non-critical
   480→notification) before any funding-path work is affected. Priority is declared per topic, not decided during an incident.
   481→Ordering honesty. Strict ordering only where it is genuinely required — per offer, per transaction, per contract.
   482→Everything else is explicitly unordered so consumers are written to tolerate it.

   505→6.5 Error handling discipline
   506→"Try/catch" is a policy here, not a reflex. The rules are explicit because inconsistent error handling is how financial
   507→systems lose money quietly.
   508→Typed errors only Every catch block handles a declared error type. A bare catch-all is permitted exactly once per
   509→service, at the top-level request boundary, where it logs with the trace ID, increments a
   510→counter, and returns internal_error.
   511→Never swallow A caught error is either handled with a declared recovery, or rethrown with added context.
   512→Logging and continuing is prohibited on any path that touches money.
   513→Fail closed on money Ambiguity on the funding path resolves to not paying, then reconciling. Ambiguity on a read
   514→path resolves to showing last-known-good with a freshness indicator.
   515→Compensation, not rollback Distributed operations use explicit compensating transactions — reverse the ledger entry, void
   516→the reservation, release the escrow hold — written and tested as first-class code paths, not as
   517→cleanup scripts.
   518→Error context Every error carries tenant, subject reference, decision ID where applicable, and trace ID. An
   519→error without context is a defect raised in code review.
   520→Timeouts everywhere No network call, database query or lock acquisition is made without an explicit timeout. A
   521→missing timeout fails the build via lint rule.
   522→6.6 Circuit breakers and bulkheads
   523→breaker(dependency):
   524→closed → open when error_rate > 50% over 20 requests in 10s
   525→or p99 latency > 3x baseline for 30s
   526→open → half after 30s cooldown
   527→half → closed after 5 consecutive successes
   528→half → open on any failure
   529→bulkheads: dupay=40 conns aani=40 openfinance=20 einvoice=15
   530→custodian=10 notification=25 (per zone, independent pools)
   531→Each dependency has an isolated connection pool and concurrency limit, so an Open Finance provider degrading
   532→cannot exhaust the pool that payouts need. Breaker state is exposed on the status endpoint and drives the UI's
   533→degrade messaging directly, so what the SME sees is derived from reality rather than guessed.

   539→The cases below are specified because they are the ones that produce either a loss or a

   541→INVOICE AND VERIFICATION
   542→Buyer amends after funding Original invoice is superseded, not edited. If the amount falls below the advance, the shortfall is
   543→recovered under the assignment terms disclosed at acceptance; the SME is notified before any
   544→recovery, never after.
   545→Credit note issued post-funding Netted against the receivable. If it exceeds the outstanding balance, the excess becomes a
   546→recoverable with a payment plan rather than an immediate demand.
   547→Partial payment by buyer Applied to the funded portion first. The remainder stays open; the invoice does not close until fully
   548→settled, and the SME sees both figures.
   549→Buyer pays the SME directly Detected by Open Finance inflow matching. SME is notified with a clear repayment instruction and
   550→a grace window before any fee. Repeat occurrences reduce the advance rate via the rule pack.
   551→Duplicate across tenants Both submissions freeze. Neither is auto-rejected. Routed to fraud review with both tenants notified
   552→that verification is in progress — never accused.
   553→Invoice currency not AED Rejected in Phase 1 with an explicit reason. FX is out of scope and the product says so rather than
   554→silently mishandling it.
   555→Due date in the past at Rejected as overdue with a reason, and routed to collection support rather than financing.
   556→submission
   557→Buyer entity dissolved Registry check at verification and re-check at settlement. Funding blocked; existing exposure
   558→moves to recovery with the SME informed immediately.
   559→PAYMENT AND SETTLEMENT
   560→Payout timeout, outcome Never retried blind. Status is polled using the deterministic idempotency key until terminal. The

   562→Payout succeeds, callback lost Reconciliation detects the orphan within the cycle and completes the state transition. The ledger is
   563→authoritative; the provider callback is a convenience.

   657→PART 9
   658→Backtesting and model governance
   659→9.1 Shadow pricing
   660→Before any rule pack prices a real dirham it is evaluated against twelve months of du's historical invoice and payment
   661→data, producing for every historical invoice the limit and price the candidate pack would have set and the outcome
   662→that actually occurred.
   663→Outputs Approval rate by segment, price distribution, expected versus realised loss, concentration
   664→profile, and the count of decisions that would have changed versus the active pack.
   665→Acceptance A candidate must not increase modelled loss beyond the declared band, must not reduce
   666→approvals in any protected segment without a documented rationale, and must explain every
   667→outcome change by a named rule.
   668→Live shadow After historical shadow passes, the candidate runs in parallel on live traffic for 30 days,
   669→deciding nothing and recording everything.
   670→9.2 Forecast backtesting
   671→Method Walk-forward validation with an expanding window. No random splits — cash flow is a time
   672→series and random splits leak the future, which is the most common way a forecast looks good
   673→and performs badly.
   674→Horizons 7, 14, 30 and 90 days, each scored separately because they are used for different decisions.
   675→Metrics MAPE and weighted MAPE on inflow; pinball loss at the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles, since
   676→the product uses the lower tail for the sweep decision; obligation-detection recall, where a miss
   677→is far costlier than a false positive.
   678→Segmented reporting By sector, size band and tenure. An aggregate number conceals exactly the segments where
   679→the model is unfit, so aggregate-only reporting is not accepted.
   680→Champion-challenger A challenger must beat the champion on the primary metric across at least four of five sectors
   681→and not regress materially on any, over two consecutive evaluation windows.
   682→9.3 Drift and monitoring
   683→Feature drift: population stability index on every input feature, weekly. PSI above 0.25 opens an investigation
   684→automatically.
   685→Calibration drift: realised against predicted, weekly, by segment. Sustained degradation widens the treasury
   686→safety margin automatically before anyone intervenes.
   687→Outcome drift: realised loss against modelled band, continuously. A breach triggers mode M1 without human
   688→delay.
   689→Fairness dispersion: approval and price distribution by sector, size and emirate each wave, with unexplained
   690→dispersion raised to the risk committee as a standing agenda item.
   691→Replay regression: nightly job replays a stratified sample of historical decisions against their recorded pack
   692→version and asserts byte-identical outcomes. Any divergence fails the build and blocks promotion.
